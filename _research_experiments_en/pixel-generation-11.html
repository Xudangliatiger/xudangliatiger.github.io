01 / Research question

The issue is not only reconstruction error, but whether a decoder rewrites the pixel distribution

The final stage of a latent generator must convert low-dimensional tokens or feature maps back to RGB. If a decoder emits pixels through fixed patches, unpatchification, or repeated upsampling, it may repeat the same filter response at spatial periods absent from the training images. The effect can alter the phase distribution of gradients and high-frequency energy even when no obvious blocks are visible.

Rather than starting from FID or training a new detector, we ask three more basic questions: does such a lattice shift exist; can it be localized to the decoder; and does one image contain enough statistics to detect or attribute its source?

Testable hypothesis

If a learned decoder causes the shift, latent generation and oracle reconstruction using that decoder should share a fingerprint. PixelDiT[2], with no separate decoder, should remain closer to Real, while distinct decoder architectures should produce different exact-frequency responses.

02 / Data and method

One pixel-space statistic covers direct generation, latent generation, and five reconstruction controls

CohortRoleSampling and pairing
Real ImageNet[1]Natural-image controlRandom 1,000 images covering 650 classes
PixelDiT[2]Direct pixel-space generationOne image for each of 1,000 classes; independent
ORT[3]Latent AR + AliTok[4] decoderOne image for each of 1,000 classes; independent
AliTok[4] / Cosmos[5] / SD VAE[6] / SDXL[7] / RAE[8]Oracle reconstruction controlsThe same 1,000 source images as Real; image-paired

For each 256×256 image, we first compute absolute Laplacian energy. At period P, coordinates are grouped by (u mod P, v mod P) and normalized by the image-wide mean to obtain a per-image phase map MP:

\[M_P(a,b)=\frac{\mathbb E[|L(x)|\mid u\bmod P=a,\ v\bmod P=b]}{\mathbb E[|L(x)|]},\qquad W_P(u,v)=M_P(u\bmod P,v\bmod P)-1.\]

WP only tiles the cohort-level expected phase-energy bias back into image coordinates to expose repetition. It is not an exact RGB watermark recovered from one image. The primary single-image feature is f(x)=vec[M₂,M₄,M₈,M₁₆], with 340 dimensions; P32 is reserved for robustness.

This definition has a direct but non-equivalent relationship to Neighboring Pixel Relationships (NPR)[10]. NPR's 2×2 blocks follow the common l=2 upsampling scale / stride, not the subsequent convolution's 3×3 kernel; NPR computes local RGB reference differences within fixed, non-overlapping 2×2 blocks. In contrast, MP aggregates modulo-P absolute-Laplacian phase energy across the full image and does not retain NPR's blockwise RGB relative values.

The two representations nevertheless share one methodological risk: a fixed block origin or coordinate origin preserves absolute grid phase. Direct P2/P4/P8/P16 concatenation can therefore form a multi-scale NPR-like phase cue without establishing four independent mechanisms. The current experiments do not confirm that this confound drives performance; cyclic shifts, random grid origins, phase-marginalized features, and an explicit NPR baseline remain uncompleted phase-origin controls.

The statistical protocol uses deterministic five-fold grouped CV. The same source image in Real and all five reconstructions always remains in one fold; PixelDiT and ORT are allocated by their 1,000 class IDs. Every scaler, centroid, and OAS covariance estimate is fitted only on the training portion of the active fold, producing complete out-of-fold predictions.

More importantly, the full ORT and AliTok-reconstruction P16 Laplacian fingerprints have correlation ρ=0.996. Their semantic sources differ: one cohort is generated by latent AR, while the other reconstructs real ImageNet images. Their key shared component is the AliTok decoder. This control localizes the strong P16 pattern from the “whole ORT generator” to the shared decoder output mechanism.

Supported causal reading

The evidence strongly supports the AliTok decoder as the direct source of ORT's P16 fingerprint. It does not support the claim that every patch embedding must create the same shift: PixelDiT also uses patch representations internally, but has no separate image decoder and remains statistically close to Real.

Eight-way balanced accuracy is the mean recall over eight sources; it is not “63.2% confidence” on one image. In the confusion matrix, ORT→AliTok=41.3% and AliTok→ORT=35.9%. This structure is exactly what shared decoding predicts, rather than random classifier failure.

Deep Image Fingerprint (DIF)[11] provides an adjacent but broader forensic framing: it learns known-generator fingerprints and tests whether fine-tuned generators retain a source fingerprint for model-lineage analysis. Our method and task are narrower. We use a predefined 340-dimensional phase statistic and report closed-set OOF attribution over eight known cohorts; there is no unknown-generator / open-set protocol and no fine-tuned model chain. The ORT/AliTok correlation and confusion therefore constitute shared-decoder, component-level provenance evidence, not model lineage in the DIF sense.

07 / Scales and CNNs

The weaker SD-family signal is not evidence that “CNNs erase fingerprints”

Left: decoder exact-frequency shifts. Middle: one-vs-Real AUC for SD and SDXL. Right: eight-way balanced accuracy. Dashed lines mark the corresponding chance levels.

Cosmos is a direct counterexample: it also uses a convolutional ResNet and upsampling, yet is almost perfectly detectable from P4–P16. SD VAE and SDXL also share their broad topology while reaching all-scale AUCs of 0.819 and 0.693. CNN is therefore not a sufficient explanation for weak fingerprints; the specific upsampling/filter response, objective, data, and trained weights also matter.

NPR[10] and DIF[11] both support CNN / upsampling architecture as an artifact prior whose traces can persist in output images; EXP-11 does not reject that prior. What the present controls establish is narrower: architecture family alone does not determine fingerprint amplitude, dominant period, or single-image detectability. These quantities still vary jointly with the specific operator, filters, objective, data, and trained weights.

Smaller periods improve SD-family binary detection. SD VAE rises from 0.819 at all scales to 0.832 at P≤8, with a difference CI of +0.004 to +0.022; SDXL rises from 0.693 to 0.724 at P4, with a difference CI of +0.014 to +0.048. But small periods are not a universal replacement: eight-way accuracy falls from 0.632 to 0.611 at P8 because AliTok/ORT P16 information is removed.

Mechanistically, each cell of a smaller phase map pools more image positions: a P8 cell aggregates 1,024 positions, while a P16 cell has only 256. That improves single-image signal-to-noise for weak periodic responses accumulated through repeated 2× upsampling. Conversely, P16 alone contributes 256 of the 340 concatenated dimensions, so naive concatenation can dilute subtle P2/P4/P8 signals.

08 / Evidence boundary

What is established, and what remains only architecture-consistent

  1. 01

    Observed: decoder cohorts have repeatable multiscale phase distributions

    Exact-frequency, distance, energy, and single-image OOF results agree across eight 1,000-image cohorts.

  2. 02

    Strongly supported: the AliTok decoder causes ORT's P16 fingerprint

    Generation and oracle reconstruction share a decoder and yield a P16 correlation of ρ=0.996; the PixelDiT control is weak.

  3. 03

    Unsupported: CNNs determine fingerprint strength

    Cosmos is a strongly detectable convolutional counterexample; SD and SDXL share a topology yet differ in amplitude.

  4. 04

    Not established: the tiled field is an exact RGB watermark

    WP is an expected high-frequency bias field; the single-image classifier reads noisy phase statistics.

  5. 05

    Sampling limit: generated controls lack a fully class-matched Real cohort

    The Real/reconstruction subset covers 650 classes, whereas PixelDiT/ORT have one image per 1,000 classes. A read-only audit found no prepared one-per-class Real control.

  6. 06

    Not completed: randomized decoder architecture under fixed conditions

    The present evidence is architecture-consistent. Upsampling or output heads have not been changed while fixing latent inputs, loss, capacity, and reconstruction quality.

  7. 07

    Method and novelty boundary: nested periods share a fixed phase origin

    P2/P4/P8/P16 are nested and origin-aligned statistics, so they cannot be described as four independent causal mechanisms. Phase-origin controls and fixed-latent decoder surgery are both incomplete.

09 / Current verdict

One advantage of pixel generation is that images need not pass through a decoder with its own lattice distribution

These experiments turn the original “patchify artifact” intuition into reproducible distribution evidence. A learned decoder can imprint architecture-specific, multiscale, and partly single-image-detectable phase fingerprints in RGB space. PixelDiT is not exactly identically distributed to Real, but its effect size under this metric is tiny; ORT and its AliTok oracle reconstruction instead share a strong, almost identical P16 pattern.

Current verdict

The evidence supports a separate latent decoder as an additional source of pixel-distribution shift, providing a principled advantage for direct pixel-space generation. It does not establish that every latent generator must be worse in perceptual quality, semantics, or FID.

The next step does not require training a new generator. First, add explicit NPR / DIF baselines and measure dependence on absolute phase through cyclic shifts, random grid origins, and phase-marginalized features. Next, compare naive concatenation, equal-block fusion, and calibrated max-over-P on the existing OOF features so P16 dimensionality does not dominate. Only after these phase-origin controls should fixed-latent decoder surgery change the upsampling or output head to create a stricter architecture-level causal control. If existing data permit, add a one-per-class Real control and unseen decoder families to test cross-dataset and cross-architecture generalization.
