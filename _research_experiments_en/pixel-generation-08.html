01 / Error origin

L0 selects the mode; fine-level temperature only moves between noise and averaging

At a fixed v0.17.7 checkpoint, the relatively stable no-CFG visual range is around temperature 0.70–0.75. Higher values add color and local variation but quickly become block noise and salt-and-pepper artifacts. Lower values suppress sampling and make structure conservative and blurred.

Per-level sweeps sharpen the conclusion. Holding L0 fixed while heating L1/L2 mostly adds unstructured high-frequency noise. Cooling L1/L2 to 0.50 / 0.25 removes grain but does not turn uncertain boundaries into coherent fine edges.

Current reading

Temperature adjusts stochasticity inside the existing distribution. It cannot replace missing child-pixel coupling or make a fine level overturn an incorrect coarse mode.

02 / GT forcing

Correcting the first level stabilizes the hierarchy, but errors still accumulate

The GT-L0 diagnostic replaces the first sampled RGB level with its true coarse target and lets later levels generate freely at temperature=0.75. Semantics, color, and layout become substantially more stable, locating much of the free-run uncertainty at L0.

When the first four levels of the five-level model are forced to GT, final L16 can produce a result close to the real layout. The final head can condition on a clean prefix; normal inference fails primarily because history drifts across levels.

  1. 01

    GT L0 strongly improves the global mode

    The first level controls identity, dominant color, and layout more than fine-level temperature does.

  2. 02

    GT history plus sampled L0 still amplifies local errors

    Even with correct positions, a wrong L0 value can expand through L1 and L2.

  3. 03

    The final level works with a correct prefix

    This points to sampled-history robustness and coarse calibration, not simply deleting fine levels.

03 / Depth and cost

Five levels add positions but no clear structure at the matched step

Matched 40k diagnostic: three levels take about 25.01s and five take about 42.36s, or 1.69× slower in this setup, without a clear semantic gain from L8/L16.

The 1.69× ratio is not a general systems claim. It applies only to this script, hardware, and sampling configuration. The narrower supported conclusion is that two extra fully sampled levels materially increase dependency depth and latency without a corresponding visual gain at the same checkpoint step.

04 / Fine residuals

The true detail exists; the generated hierarchy increasingly underestimates it

The GT multiresolution pyramid uses the model's exact target construction: patch-wise area downsampling, 8-bit rounding, then nearest upsampling for display. Adjacent-level residuals contain contours, fur, text, building edges, and thin texture, ruling out the idea that late targets contain no information.

05 / Objectives

Focal loss finds hard pixels, but does not establish the main bottleneck

Teacher-forced DMoL NLL maps align with edges and high-frequency regions. v0.17.8.1 therefore uses per-channel probability for focal weighting:

\[p_i=e^{-\mathrm{NLL}_i/3},\quad w_i=\mathrm{stopgrad}(1-p_i)^2,\quad \mathcal L=\frac{\sum_i w_i\mathrm{NLL}_i}{\sum_i w_i+\epsilon}.\]

Visual quality remains broadly similar after focal reweighting. The loss can identify hard regions, but controlled evidence of better final generation is absent.

Blur history, noisy history, and a hot-L0/cool-fine schedule are also reasonable hypotheses, not completed improvements. The next record therefore changes structure more strongly: concentrate stochastic choice in one patch-level AR process and let later cascade decoders perform conditional refinement.
